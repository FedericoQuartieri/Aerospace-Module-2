\section{Verification and results}
\label{sec:risultati}

\subsection{Method}
\label{sec:metodo}

Every case of the reference work was computed with the corresponding
zero-dimensional program. The pure-nitrogen cases and the molecular-atomic
nitrogen mixture, both without reactions and with reactions starting from a
non-equilibrium state, were also computed with the solver, on a single-cell
mesh, with the same initial conditions, the same data and the same time step of
one nanosecond. The results are subjected to three checks:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item \textbf{solver against zero-dimensional programs}: the two paths
    implement the same physics, so any difference can only come from the way
    the solver integrates the model; this check verifies the implementation;
  \item \textbf{zero-dimensional programs against the reference work}:
    this check verifies the physics;
  \item \textbf{final temperature against energy conservation}: the
    equilibrium temperature must match both the value obtained from the initial
    energy and the value reported in the reference work.
\end{enumerate}

The \hyfoam{} curves were extracted directly from the PDF of the reference work. The
figures are vector graphics, in which each curve is stored as a sequence of
points; these were read and converted to time and temperature using the axis
ticks. The uncertainty is of the order of the line width, about 1\% on the
temperatures. Deviations, both on the temperatures and on the species
densities, are given as percentages and represent the maximum over the
transient. The programs write their results every 10 or 100~ns, and the
comparisons are based on these samples. In the plots, the light solid lines
show the reference work, the dashed lines the zero-dimensional programs, and
the dots the solver.

\subsection{Pure nitrogen}
\label{sec:risultati-azoto}

In the first two cases the nitrogen is at atmospheric pressure, with a
vibrational temperature initially much lower than the translational one
(vibrational heating) or much higher (vibrational cooling). In the heating
case the final temperature matches the value given in the text of the reference work,
and the curves differ by at most 0.3\% on the translational temperature and
1.5\% on the vibrational temperature. In the cooling case the final
temperature differs by 0.03\%; the deviation during the transient rises to
2.8\% and 5.1\% respectively, reflecting a slightly delayed relaxation
(Figure~\ref{fig:azoto-3}).

The third case starts from a much higher translational temperature, at which
the electronic energy is no longer negligible, and compares the gas without and
with electronic energy (Figure~\ref{fig:azoto-4}). In both cases the final
temperature matches the value reported in the reference work to within 0.3\%, and the
electronic energy lowers it by about 20\%. The deviations during the transient
are 0.05\% and 0.8\% without electronic energy, and 0.3\% and 4\% with it.
This case justifies the decision to reformulate the V--T exchange in terms of
the vibro-electronic energy: with the
original \mpp{} formulation, which uses the vibrational energy only, the final
temperature would be the same, but the deviations during the transient would
rise to 28\% and 56\%.

\subsection{Non-reacting mixtures}
\label{sec:risultati-miscele}

The first case is an equimolar mixture of molecular and atomic nitrogen, with
a very high translational temperature and a low vibrational temperature. The
final temperature matches that of the reference work to within 0.2\%, and the
translational temperature remains within 2.5\%. The vibrational temperature,
however, rises later than in the reference work, with a deviation of up to
20\% on the steepest part of the curve. The most likely cause is the
relaxation time in mixtures, which \mpp{} averages differently from the
reference work: vibrational relaxation
is somewhat slower, while the final state is correct.

The second case is an equimolar nitrogen-oxygen mixture at atmospheric
pressure, with a vibrational temperature much higher than the translational
one, computed both without and with V--V exchange between the two molecules.
The final temperature matches that of the reference work to within 0.3\%. Without V--V
exchange the deviations are 2.7\% on the translational temperature, 2.7\% on
the vibrational temperature of nitrogen and 6.4\% on that of oxygen; with the
exchange they drop to 2.0\%, 2.5\% and 3.4\%. As in the reference work, the
V--V exchange brings the two vibrational temperatures closer together and
speeds up the return to equilibrium (Figure~\ref{fig:miscele}).

\subsection{Reacting mixtures}
\label{sec:risultati-reagenti}

The molecular-atomic nitrogen mixture was also computed with nitrogen
dissociation, starting from two different initial states. In the first, out of
equilibrium as in the non-reacting case, the two temperatures at the end of
the computation match those of the reference work to within 0.4\%, and the composition
follows that of the reference work to within 1.4\% throughout
(Figure~\ref{fig:reagenti}). The translational temperature remains within
2.3\%, while the vibrational temperature shows the same delay as in the
non-reacting mixture, with deviations of up to 17\% at early times. The second
case starts with equal temperatures, i.e.\ in thermal but not chemical
equilibrium: the final temperatures match those of the reference work to within 0.8\%,
the composition remains within 2.3\%, and the temperatures during the
transient within 2\% and 10\%.

These cases also confirm the choice of exponent in Park's temperature. With
the fixed \mpp{} value ($a = 0.5$), the composition in the first case deviates
from the reference work by up to 9\% and the translational temperature by up
to 6.7\%; with $a = 0.7$, as in the reference work, the deviations drop to
1.4\% and 2.3\%. The vibrational temperature is unaffected, because its
deviation stems from the relaxation rather than from the chemistry.

\subsection{Five-species air}
\label{sec:risultati-aria}

The last case is air (79\% nitrogen and 21\% oxygen by mole) at low pressure
and high temperature, with nineteen reactions and a single temperature. With
the Quantum-Kinetic rate constants, the temperature follows the reference work
to within 0.7\% and its final value matches to within 0.4\%; the densities of
the five species remain within 15\%, and four of the five within 7\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:aria}). With the Park mechanism of \mpp, on the other hand,
the temperature deviates by up to 8.9\%, and the densities of molecular and
atomic oxygen, atomic nitrogen and nitric oxide differ by factors between 1.6
and 3. The results reported in the reference work for this case were therefore obtained
with the Quantum-Kinetic rate constants.

\subsection{Solver versus zero-dimensional programs}
\label{sec:risultati-solver}

Table~\ref{tab:solver} lists the maximum deviation between the solver and the
zero-dimensional programs for all the cases computed with both. The final
temperatures coincide, and the curves agree to within 0.3\% in every case except
high-temperature nitrogen, where the deviation reaches 1.2\% during the
very rapid relaxation of the first 100~ns. In this regime the time-integration
scheme matters more: the solver performs two iterations per time step, whereas
the zero-dimensional programs perform only one. Even with reactions, the
composition agrees to within 0.03\%. The model is therefore correctly
integrated into the solver, chemistry included.
