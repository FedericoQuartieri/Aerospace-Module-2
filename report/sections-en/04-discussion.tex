\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussione}

\subsection{Interpretation of the results}
\label{sec:discussione-risultati}

Agreement between the solver and the zero-dimensional programs supports the
correct integration of the model into OpenFOAM. Differences remain within
0.3\% for the cases evaluated with both approaches, including chemical
reactions, except for high-temperature nitrogen. Comparison with the reference
study further assesses the thermochemical model. In non-reacting cases, final
temperatures are consistent with energy conservation and agree with the reference values to
within 0.3\%, which is within the uncertainty associated with extracting data
from the figures. In the reacting cases, the final temperatures agree to
within 0.5\%, the nitrogen-mixture composition to within 1.7\% and the air
composition to within 15\%. Transient temperature differences remain within
6\% in every case. The largest occur in the vibrational temperatures of pure
nitrogen during vibrational cooling (5.1\%) and of oxygen in the
nitrogen-oxygen mixture (5.6\%); in the molecular-atomic nitrogen mixtures,
with and without reactions, they remain below 0.5\%.

\subsection{Sources of the discrepancies}
\label{sec:discussione-origine}

With the relaxation-time formula of \mpp{}, the rise in vibrational
temperature in molecular-atomic nitrogen is delayed, with deviations of up to
19.6\% without reactions and 20.0\% with them. With the formula of the
reference study, the deviations fall to 0.44\% and 0.31\%, respectively
(Figure~\ref{fig:sensibilita-tau}). The two formulas differ mainly in Park's
correction, which dominates the relaxation time at these temperatures, rather
than in the averaging over collision partners. For the collision between
molecular and atomic nitrogen, \mpp{} evaluates the correction with the number
density of the vibrating molecule alone, whereas the reference study uses the
number density of the colliding pair. At the initial temperature of
30\,000~K, the relaxation time of \mpp{} is therefore 38\% longer, while the
choice between the arithmetic average of \mpp{} and the harmonic average of
the reference study changes it by less than 0.1\%. Pure nitrogen is
unaffected, since the two formulas coincide for a single species, and so is
the equilibrium state, which is determined by the conserved energy and the
thermodynamic model. The reference study attributes its difference from
LeMANS for the same mixture to a third convention for this number density,
that of the whole mixture. For pure nitrogen and the nitrogen-oxygen mixture,
temporal shifts of a few percent remain, and their origin has not been
isolated.

\subsection{Methodological implications}
\label{sec:discussione-lezioni}

Reproducing the reference results required reconstructing modelling choices
that the reference study leaves unspecified or describes only partially, and
replacing the default formulations of \mpp{} where they differ from it.
These include the treatment of electronic energy, the chemistry--vibration
coupling, the relaxation-time formula, the energy used in the relaxation
source term, the exponent in Park's dissociation temperature and the
reaction-rate constants for air. The comparisons collected in
Section~\ref{sec:risultati-sensibilita} show that these choices can produce
deviations larger than the residual differences: 20\% in vibrational
temperature, a factor of 3.5 in air species densities and 25\% in final
temperature. The reference study also illustrates this sensitivity by
attributing its difference from LeMANS for molecular-atomic nitrogen to a
different convention in Park's correction, the same term that separates the
formula of \mpp{} from that of the reference study
(Section~\ref{sec:discussione-origine}).

Two complementary checks guided the selection of these modelling assumptions.
First, the equilibrium temperature obtained from energy conservation is
independent of the relaxation model and provides a check on the thermodynamic
data and energy conventions. Second, the curves extracted from the vector
figures enable a quantitative comparison with the reference study. Because the
solver and zero-dimensional programs share the source-term functions, their
agreement alone cannot exclude errors in those functions; the reference
curves and equilibrium checks provide complementary
evidence.

\subsection{From a single cell to a full mesh}
\label{sec:discussione-multicella}

The results presented here come from a single cell with no flow. A real
simulation has many cells, and the gas moves from one to the next; three
additions are needed. First, the vibro-electronic energy equation, which now
contains only the source terms, must gain a convective term, built with the
same face fluxes already used for the species, and a conduction term. Second,
the two new fields need boundary conditions: the vibro-electronic temperature
is read from the case and already has them, whereas the vibro-electronic
energy is obtained from the temperature only inside the domain, so its values
on the boundary faces must be obtained in the same way; walls and inlets also
need a rule for the vibro-electronic temperature, which a closed heat bath
does not require. Third, the source terms use the density left by the previous
pressure update, which is exact at constant volume but should be replaced by
the density of the current step once the gas can compress.
