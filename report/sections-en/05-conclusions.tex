\section{Conclusions and future work}
\label{sec:conclusioni}

\subsection{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusioni-sintesi}

This work implemented a two-temperature thermochemical model in OpenFOAM-13
as a basis for simulations of reacting hypersonic flows. The solver advances
the energy equations and uses \mpp{} to recover the temperatures and evaluate
thermochemical properties. The source-term functions are shared with
stand-alone zero-dimensional programs. Where the library's default
formulations differ from those of the reference study, as for the relaxation
time, the energy driving the V--T exchange, Park's temperature and the
chemistry--vibration coupling, customised source terms have been implemented
using the properties supplied by \mpp.

All zero-dimensional verification cases reported by Casseau
\textit{et al.}~\cite{casseau2016} have been reproduced; for the reacting
nitrogen mixture, the Park configuration was reproduced, whereas the
configuration based on the coupled vibration-dissociation-vibration (CVDV)
model was not implemented. Across the cases
computed with both implementations, including reacting cases, the solver
agrees with the zero-dimensional programs to within 0.3\%, except for
high-temperature nitrogen, where the maximum difference is 1.2\%.
Compared with the reference study, final temperatures agree to within 0.5\%,
nitrogen-mixture composition to within 1.7\% and air composition to within
15\%. With the relaxation-time formula of \mpp{}, the vibrational temperature
in mixtures containing atomic nitrogen rises with a delay, with deviations of
up to 20\%; with the formula of the reference study they fall below 0.5\%.
The difference lies in the number density used in Park's correction, the
same term to which the reference study attributes its difference from
LeMANS.

Reproduction of the results required reconstructing incompletely specified
modelling choices, particularly the treatment of electronic energy and the
chemistry--vibration coupling, and retrieving the rate constants for air,
which the reference study gives only through a bibliographic reference.
Verification remains limited to spatially
uniform heat baths with no flow; flow applications require the extensions
outlined below.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:sviluppi}

The proposed extensions address multi-cell operation, the transport of
energy, viscous-flow modelling and the thermodynamic properties used by the
solver. The first is the decisive one and relies on the other three.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item \textbf{Running on a real mesh.} Everything has been verified on a
    single cell, and this is the step that makes the model usable on a real
    flow. What it requires is described in
    Section~\ref{sec:discussione-multicella}: boundary conditions for the two
    new fields, the source terms evaluated with the density of the current
    step, the transport terms of the next point and the thermodynamic
    properties of the last one. Every computation of the thermophysical model
    is local to the cell.
  \item \textbf{Transport of the vibro-electronic energy.} This is the part
    of the previous point that can be developed and tested on its own. The
    face fluxes needed for the convective term are those already computed for
    the species, while the conductive term uses the vibro-electronic
    conductivity of the next point.
  \item \textbf{Two-temperature viscosity and thermal conductivity.} These are
    required for viscous flows and for the wall heat flux. \mpp{} already
    computes them, and splits the conductivity into its translational and
    vibro-electronic contributions: they only need to be queried cell by cell
    in the thermophysical model, as is currently done for the energies, and
    passed to the solver in place of the values computed at start-up.
  \item \textbf{Updating the thermodynamic properties.} The heat capacities
    $C_p$ and $C_v$, the viscosity and the thermal conductivity are computed
    once, at start-up, by the one-temperature model of OpenFOAM, and are then
    never updated, because the thermophysical model replaces the update step
    in order to recover the two temperatures. They play no role in the
    uniform heat baths, but in a flow the ratio $C_p/C_v$ enters the speed of
    sound used by the numerical fluxes. They should be recomputed at every
    update, with \mpp{} at the two temperatures.
\end{enumerate}
